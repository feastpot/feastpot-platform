FEASTPOT LAUNCH READINESS AUDIT
17 September 2026

Purpose
-------
This audit assessed launch readiness for customers, vendors and staff at
commit 9c03ecad89f258adad72cb9e768d75b53b03d4eb. It covered the
repository build, type checks, lint, dependencies, tests, all application
route inventories, customer browser rendering, targeted vendor and Admin
tests, uploads, links, rates, money, food safety, terms, notifications and
security.

The audit used current source, local running services, real Chromium visits,
read-only database queries and commands recorded in audit/00-inventory.md
through audit/08-critical.md. It changed no product code, restarted no
service, pushed nothing, ran no CI or deployment, and did not replay or
discard queue jobs.

Verdict
-------
Feastpot is not ready for real vendors, customers or staff.
Notification delivery is already severely backed up.
Catering quotes use the wrong commission source and can charge 8%, not 10%.
The effective vendor terms have 19 acceptances for 277 live development
vendors, and production acceptance coverage is unknown.
The real customer purchase, empty-vendor journey and Admin operations are
not currently provable with the repository's own browser suites.
HEIC is unsupported and private-document safety was not demonstrated.
Ten high dependency vulnerabilities include the upload-processing stack.
All seven blockers below must be closed and re-audited before launch.

Blockers - Must Fix Before Any Real User
-----------------------------------------
1. Notification delivery is already severely backed up.
Why this matters: Customers, vendors and staff may not receive order,
payment, compliance or legal notices. Some failures may be permanent.
Evidence: The API log recorded notifications failed=135 and waiting=254.
It also recorded 12 failed compliance jobs and 8 failed terms-notice jobs.
The log said QUEUE_ALERT_SLACK_WEBHOOK_URL was not set.
See audit/00-inventory.md:216-232.
What to implement: Diagnose and clear the causes without discarding
evidence, restore timely processing, configure a monitored alert channel,
and prove a real order confirmation reaches its provider.

2. Catering quotes use the marketplace-first 8% rate instead of 10%.
Why this matters: Every new catering quote can record the wrong platform
commission and vendor payout.
Evidence:
apps/api/src/modules/catering-bookings/catering-bookings.service.ts:148-165
sets OrderSource.MARKETPLACE and isFirstOrder=true before calculation.
The canonical catering rate is 10% at
packages/config/src/commission-rates.ts:21-25.
What to implement: Route catering calculations through the catering rate
source, migrate or reconcile affected quotes, and add a test that fails if a
catering quote receives any marketplace rate.

3. Effective vendor terms acceptance is not reconciled for launch data.
Why this matters: A vendor may trade without accepting the terms that govern
fees, safety duties, suspension, complaints and payouts.
Evidence: A read-only query selected effective vendor terms version
part-b-1788799279281. It counted 19 distinct acceptances and 277 vendors
marked live. See audit/08-critical.md under Food safety and legal.
What to implement: Separate real and test vendors, verify the same counts in
production, prevent unaccepted vendors from trading, and obtain current
acceptance from every real live vendor before launch.

4. The real customer purchase journey is not launch-verified.
Why this matters: The platform cannot take orders safely if discovery,
pricing, payment, confirmation, history or cancellation fails.
Evidence: The customer Playwright run reported 37 failed, 8 skipped,
31 did not run and 51 passed in 193.053 seconds. Failures included checkout
financials, payment outcomes, the 375 px post-order journey and the real
Stripe purchase smoke. See audit/03-customer.md.
What to implement: Repair the shared test prerequisites and every genuine
journey defect, then require one clean test-mode purchase from postcode
search through provider payment, confirmation, history and cancellation.

5. Zero-data Vendor and authenticated Admin operations are not certified.
Why this matters: A new cook or staff member can encounter an unusable
portal at the exact point support and operational control are most needed.
Evidence: The Vendor suite authenticated V4, then deleted its shared session
file and its dependent projects failed in milliseconds. The Admin setup was
redirected to /unauthorized?reason=mfa-configuration. The Admin route map
also omits /platform-facts. See audit/04-vendor.md and
audit/05-admin.md.
What to implement: Make both suites deterministic, align the MFA test
configuration with production policy, add /platform-facts to the enforced
role inventory, and produce clean route and role matrices for zero-data V4
and all four staff roles.

6. Upload handling is not safe or usable enough for launch.
Why this matters: Normal iPhone photos fail, spoofed private documents can
reach storage, old objects accumulate, and cross-account privacy has not been
proven.
Evidence: The bucket MIME lists exclude HEIC at
apps/api/src/modules/catalogue/supabase-storage.service.ts:65-76.
Compliance and dispute services upload declared bytes without magic checks
at apps/api/src/modules/compliance/compliance.service.ts:75-100 and
apps/api/src/modules/disputes/disputes.service.ts:841-875.
No EXIF orientation handling was found. See audit/06-uploads.md.
What to implement: Accept and safely normalise HEIC, validate real content
before storage, reject empty and active content, normalise orientation,
delete superseded objects, and prove signed-out and cross-vendor denial.

7. The dependency tree contains ten high vulnerabilities.
Why this matters: Multer and Sharp are on user-controlled upload paths.
Their advisories include denial of service, file-size bypass and image
processing weaknesses.
Evidence: npm audit exited 1 with 13 vulnerabilities: 10 high and 3
moderate. High packages included multer and sharp.
See audit/01-static.md:86-114.
What to implement: Upgrade or replace affected packages, resolve the
framework-breaking upgrade path deliberately, rerun the upload abuse matrix,
and require a clean or formally risk-accepted dependency audit.

Fix Within The First Weeks
--------------------------
1. Current automated coverage gives misleading confidence.
Why this matters: Regressions can pass while critical integrations never run.
Evidence: API tests reported 283 skipped. Current API coverage did not finish
within five minutes. Web branch coverage measured 0.87%, below its 1.3%
threshold. Vendor has an echo instead of unit tests.
See audit/02-tests.md.
What to implement: Make credential-dependent suites fail clearly in the
required environment, publish coverage per workspace, raise meaningful
thresholds, and replace the Vendor echo with real tests.

2. Vendor branch-protection naming may not enforce the intended check.
Why this matters: A broken Vendor portal change could merge while the
repository appears to require a differently named check.
Evidence: Checked-in branch protection names
E2E (vendor portal, all projects), while the workflow job is named
E2E (vendor authoritative lifecycle). See audit/02-tests.md.
What to implement: Inspect actual GitHub protection, align the exact check
name, and prove a deliberately failing Vendor check blocks a test branch.

3. User interfaces can expose raw internal or provider error text.
Why this matters: Users receive confusing messages and internal details may
leak.
Evidence:
apps/vendor/src/app/menu/menu-list-client.tsx:209-214,
apps/vendor/src/app/analytics/analytics-client.tsx:53-59,
apps/vendor/src/app/payouts/payouts-client.tsx:193-197, and
apps/api/src/modules/compliance/compliance.service.ts:94-100.
What to implement: Map internal failures to stable user messages, retain
provider detail only in structured logs, and test each error state.

4. File replacement and partial upload failures leave orphaned objects.
Why this matters: Storage cost grows and private files outlive their visible
record.
Evidence:
apps/api/src/modules/catalogue/menu-items.service.ts:1031-1051 and
apps/api/src/modules/reviews/reviews.service.ts:203-216.
See audit/06-uploads.md for the other paths.
What to implement: Make object and database updates compensating or
transactional, remove replaced objects, and run a scheduled orphan report.

5. The build passes with warnings and the sitemap omits vendor URLs.
Why this matters: Vendor discovery from search engines is incomplete and
warning noise hides real regressions.
Evidence: The build passed 5/5 tasks but every frontend compiled with
warnings. Sitemap generation received HTTP 400 and skipped vendor URLs.
See audit/01-static.md:10-44.
What to implement: Fix the vendor sitemap request, fail build verification
when required sitemap data is missing, and reduce warnings to zero.
